# 02 — Blind Inventors, One-Eyed Investors

<small>2 min read</small>

## Core idea
Being close to an idea doesn't make you a better judge of it — it often makes you a worse one. Grant argues that creators are frequently the last people who should be trusted to evaluate their own work, because attachment, sunk effort, and the difficulty of imagining an idea any other way all cloud the judgment of the person who made it. Distance, paradoxically, sharpens evaluation.

## Why it matters
This cuts against the instinct to trust your own gut most on the ideas you care about most. If proximity degrades judgment, then the ideas you're most invested in — the ones where you'd least want to be told you're wrong — are exactly the ones where your self-assessment is least reliable. Originality, in this framing, depends less on having great personal instincts and more on building a habit of routing your best ideas through people who don't share your attachment to them.

## Example from the book
Grant draws on research into "idea selection" — the ability to predict which of your own ideas will actually succeed — using studies of creative professionals rating their own work. Across these studies, a consistent pattern shows up: creators' confidence in their own best ideas is a weak predictor of which ideas actually go on to succeed, while independent peers — people with relevant taste but no personal stake in the specific idea — turn out to be considerably better forecasters. The gap isn't a lack of expertise on the creator's part; it's that expertise and attachment are pulling in opposite directions at once.

## Practical application
Before committing to your strongest hunch, find someone who understands the domain but has no investment in defending your particular version of it, and ask them to rank your ideas — not just critique the one you already like best. The value isn't just catching flaws; it's getting an evaluation untangled from the sunk cost and identity you've already wrapped around the idea.

## Something to sit with
> [!question]- A question to think about
> Which of your current ideas are you most confident about — and is that confidence coming from evidence, or from how much of yourself you've already invested in it?
