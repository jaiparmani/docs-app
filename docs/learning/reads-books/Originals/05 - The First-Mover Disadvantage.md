# 05 — The First-Mover Disadvantage

<small>2 min read</small>

## Core idea
Being first into a market is usually treated as the biggest possible competitive edge. Grant argues the opposite is closer to true on average: pioneers fail at meaningfully higher rates than the "fast followers" who enter a category after them, because pioneers bear the cost of every mistake there was no precedent to warn them about.

## Why it matters
This challenges the instinct to rush an idea to market the moment it occurs to you, out of fear that someone else will get there first. If later entrants systematically outperform pioneers — because they get to watch the pioneer's errors, wait for the market to mature, and refine rather than invent from nothing — then speed to market is not automatically the advantage it feels like, and "someone else might beat me to it" is a weaker reason to rush than it sounds.

## Example from the book
Grant cites research comparing the survival and success rates of market pioneers against companies that entered the same categories later. Pioneers, across a range of industries, failed considerably more often than the fast followers who came after them. The pioneers had to convince a market that didn't yet understand the category to exist, absorb the cost of design and positioning mistakes with no prior example to learn from, and educate customers from scratch — costs that later entrants simply didn't have to pay, because the pioneer had already paid them.

## Practical application
When you're weighing whether to launch now or wait, separate "being first" from "having the best version of the idea." If waiting lets you watch others make the expensive early mistakes, absorb what the market actually wants, and enter with a more refined version, that patience is often a competitive advantage rather than a wasted head start.

## Something to sit with
> [!question]- A question to think about
> Where have you assumed that moving first was an advantage — and is there a version of that situation where letting someone else go first, and learning from what breaks for them, would put you in a stronger position?
