# Originals
*Adam Grant*

Grant's case that originality isn't a fixed trait belonging to fearless risk-takers — it's a set of learnable moves, from hedging your bets to holding many mediocre ideas alongside your best ones. It's an earlier, sibling book to *Think Again*: where this one is about generating and championing novel ideas, that one applies the same underlying bet — psychologically flexible thinking beats rigid conviction — to the harder skill of admitting you were wrong. Broken into 11 concepts, one per chunk, each a 3-5 minute read.

## Concepts

1. [The Risk Portfolio](01%20-%20The%20Risk%20Portfolio.md) — why the most successful originals hedge rather than go all in
2. [Blind Inventors, One-Eyed Investors](02%20-%20Blind%20Inventors%2C%20One-Eyed%20Investors.md) — why creators are often the worst judges of their own ideas
3. [Quantity Breeds Quality](03%20-%20Quantity%20Breeds%20Quality.md) — why volume, not instinct, produces great work
4. [Strategic Procrastination](04%20-%20Strategic%20Procrastination.md) — how delaying execution buys room for better ideas
5. [The First-Mover Disadvantage](05%20-%20The%20First-Mover%20Disadvantage.md) — why pioneers fail more than the improvers who follow them
6. [Vuja De](06%20-%20Vuja%20De.md) — seeing a familiar system as if for the first time
7. [The Goldilocks Pitch](07%20-%20The%20Goldilocks%20Pitch.md) — why offering options persuades better than selling one idea
8. [Rethinking Groupthink](08%20-%20Rethinking%20Groupthink.md) — why an assigned devil's advocate is a weaker safeguard than real dissent
9. [Birth Order and Rebellion](09%20-%20Birth%20Order%20and%20Rebellion.md) — why later-born children take more risks against the status quo
10. [Raising Originals](10%20-%20Raising%20Originals.md) — why fewer rules and more explained values produce more original kids
11. [Doubting the Idea, Not Yourself](11%20-%20Doubting%20the%20Idea%2C%20Not%20Yourself.md) — the distinction that separates originals who persist from those who quit
