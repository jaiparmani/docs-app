# Shoe Dog
*Phil Knight*

Nike's founding story, as Knight tells it, isn't a strategy — it's a decade-plus of near-fatal cash crises survived through improvisation, relationships, and timing that could easily have broken the other way. Broken into 11 concepts, one per chunk, following the real chronological episodes of Blue Ribbon Sports/Nike's founding through its 1980 IPO, with the recurring banking and cash-flow crisis as the actual throughline. Each chunk covers the core idea, why it matters, a specific example from the book, a practical way to apply it, and a question to sit with.

## Concepts

1. [The Crazy Idea](01%20-%20The%20Crazy%20Idea.md) — Knight's 1962 pitch to Onitsuka for a company that didn't exist yet
2. [A Handshake Instead of a Contract](02%20-%20A%20Handshake%20Instead%20of%20a%20Contract.md) — why the original Onitsuka deal was never pinned down, and what that cost later
3. [Bowerman's Waffle Iron](03%20-%20Bowerman's%20Waffle%20Iron.md) — the coach-turned-co-founder who gave the company its product credibility
4. [Naming the Company Nike](04%20-%20Naming%20the%20Company%20Nike.md) — how a deadline, not a brand process, produced the name and the swoosh
5. [Outrunning the Balance Sheet](05%20-%20Outrunning%20the%20Balance%20Sheet.md) — why growth was the company's most dangerous liability
6. [The Banker Who Could End It All](06%20-%20The%20Banker%20Who%20Could%20End%20It%20All.md) — surviving the loan officers who could have called it quits
7. [The Lifeline from Nissho Iwai](07%20-%20The%20Lifeline%20from%20Nissho%20Iwai.md) — the Japanese trading company that bankrolled survival for its own reasons
8. [When Onitsuka Turned](08%20-%20When%20Onitsuka%20Turned.md) — the rupture, the lawsuit, and the fallback brand built just in time
9. [Betting the Company Every Season](09%20-%20Betting%20the%20Company%20Every%20Season.md) — the structural, recurring mismatch that generated every crisis
10. [Going Public to Buy Peace](10%20-%20Going%20Public%20to%20Buy%20Peace.md) — why the 1980 IPO was a fix for a problem, not a victory lap
11. [The Honesty About Luck](11%20-%20The%20Honesty%20About%20Luck.md) — Knight's own candor about how much of the outcome wasn't strategy
