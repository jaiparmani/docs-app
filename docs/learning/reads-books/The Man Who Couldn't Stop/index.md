# The Man Who Couldn't Stop

*David Adam*

Adam, a science journalist who has lived with contamination-focused OCD since young adulthood, weaves his own case history through the neuroscience and social history of obsessive-compulsive disorder. Broken into 9 concepts, one per chunk, each a 3-5 minute read. Read them in order — the loop mechanism in the first chunk is the frame the rest of the book keeps returning to.

## Concepts

1. [The Compulsion-Relief Loop](01%20-%20The%20Compulsion-Relief%20Loop.md) — the self-reinforcing cycle that turns a passing thought into a disorder
2. [The Near-Universality of Intrusive Thoughts](02%20-%20The%20Near-Universality%20of%20Intrusive%20Thoughts.md) — why almost everyone has had a thought they'd never admit to
3. [Adam's Own Story — Contamination and Doubt](03%20-%20Adam's%20Own%20Story%20-%20Contamination%20and%20Doubt.md) — how the author's HIV-contamination fear took hold
4. [The Worry Circuit in the Brain](04%20-%20The%20Worry%20Circuit%20in%20the%20Brain.md) — the neuroscience of a threat signal that won't switch off
5. [From Scrupulosity to Diagnosis](05%20-%20From%20Scrupulosity%20to%20Diagnosis.md) — how OCD went from a moral failing to a treatable circuit problem
6. [Samuel Johnson's Compulsive Rituals](06%20-%20Samuel%20Johnson's%20Compulsive%20Rituals.md) — a historical case study of the disorder before it had a name
7. [Darwin's Illness and Anxiety](07%20-%20Darwin's%20Illness%20and%20Anxiety.md) — a second historical case study, and the limits of retrospective diagnosis
8. [Exposure and Response Prevention](08%20-%20Exposure%20and%20Response%20Prevention.md) — the therapy that works by deliberately not resolving the anxiety
9. [The Spectrum Claim — Useful Insight, Easy Misuse](09%20-%20The%20Spectrum%20Claim%20-%20Useful%20Insight%2C%20Easy%20Misuse.md) — why "everyone's a little OCD" is both the book's best idea and its most misused one
