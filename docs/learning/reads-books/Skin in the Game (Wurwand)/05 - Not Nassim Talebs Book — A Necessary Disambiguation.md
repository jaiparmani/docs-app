# 05 — Not Nassim Taleb's Book: A Necessary Disambiguation

<small>2 min read</small>

## Core idea
This is worth stating plainly and on its own, not just as an aside: Jane Wurwand's *Skin in the Game: Everything You Need Is Already Inside You* is a completely different book from Nassim Nicholas Taleb's *Skin in the Game: Hidden Asymmetries in Daily Life* (2018). They share three words in the main title and nothing else. Taleb's book is a philosophical and economic work about incentive structures, risk-bearing, and asymmetry between those who make decisions and those who bear their consequences — part of his *Incerto* series. Wurwand's book is a founder memoir by the co-founder of Dermalogica, telling a personal and business origin story. There is no overlap in author, genre, argument, or intent — only a coincidental title collision.

## Why it matters
Title collisions like this are a real, practical hazard in any reading or note-taking system — a search for "skin in the game" is far more likely to surface Taleb's book, given its much wider circulation in business and rationalist reading circles, which creates real risk of misfiling notes, misattributing ideas, or recommending the wrong book to someone based on a half-remembered title. In a personal knowledge base built from many book notes across genres, this is exactly the kind of collision worth flagging explicitly rather than assuming context will sort it out.

## Example from the book
There is nothing to illustrate from either book's content here — this chunk exists specifically to prevent content from the two books being blended together in the first place. The only fact being asserted is the collision itself: same three words in the title, unrelated author, unrelated subject, unrelated genre.

## Practical application
When referencing this book in notes, conversation, or search, use the full subtitle or the author's name — "Wurwand's *Skin in the Game*" or "*Everything You Need Is Already Inside You*" — rather than the bare title, precisely because the bare title is ambiguous. The same discipline is worth applying to any book whose title overlaps with a more famous unrelated work.
