# Bulls, Bears and Other Beasts

*Santosh Nair*

A financial journalist's history of the Indian stock market, told through its major scams and cycles — Harshad Mehta, Ketan Parekh, and the regulatory and infrastructural changes that followed each one — rather than through an academic theory of crises. It's a considerably less internationally documented title than most others in this collection, so this chunked treatment leans on the well-established public history of the events it covers rather than claiming to reproduce specific passages or scenes from the book itself; see chunk 8 for that caveat in full.

1. [The Harshad Mehta Scam and the Bank Receipt Loophole](01%20-%20The%20Harshad%20Mehta%20Scam%20and%20the%20Bank%20Receipt%20Loophole.md) — how a reconciliation gap between banking and the stock market let one broker manufacture a bull run
2. [Journalism Over Theory](02%20-%20Journalism%20Over%20Theory.md) — why the book tells history through specific operators rather than abstracted frameworks

4. [Every Scam Exploits a Different Loophole](04%20-%20Every%20Scam%20Exploits%20a%20Different%20Loophole.md) — the book's implicit argument that regulation is always chasing the last failure
5. [From the Trading Floor to the Screen](05%20-%20From%20the%20Trading%20Floor%20to%20the%20Screen.md) — how electronic trading and dematerialization closed an entire category of fraud through infrastructure, not rules
6. [Building the Regulator](06%20-%20Building%20the%20Regulator.md) — how SEBI's real powers were forged scandal by scandal, not planned in advance
7. [The Retail Investor's Recurring Role](07%20-%20The%20Retail%20Investor's%20Recurring%20Role.md) — why ordinary investors are the ones left holding the loss in every cycle
