# We Should All Be Feminists

*Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie*

Adapted from Adichie's TEDx talk of the same name, this is a short, tightly-focused essay rather than a full book — closer to a fifteen-minute argument than a treatise. Broken into 6 concepts, one per chunk, each a short read built from the essay's own concrete scenes rather than abstraction. Read them in order — the closing chunk builds directly on the five before it.

## Concepts

1. [Gender as a Training Program](01%20-%20Gender%20as%20a%20Training%20Program.md) — how childhood rules get taught before anyone's old enough to question them
2. [The Word Doesn't Need Softening](02%20-%20The%20Word%20Doesn't%20Need%20Softening.md) — why "feminist" resists being replaced with something vaguer
3. [Biology vs. the Rules Built on Top of It](03%20-%20Biology%20vs.%20the%20Rules%20Built%20on%20Top%20of%20It.md) — a small physical difference versus a large inherited hierarchy
4. [The Cost of Masculinity](04%20-%20The%20Cost%20of%20Masculinity.md) — what rigid strength costs the people required to perform it
5. [Marriage, Anger, and the Pressure to Be Liked](05%20-%20Marriage%2C%20Anger%2C%20and%20the%20Pressure%20to%20Be%20Liked.md) — how ambition gets quietly discounted against being liked
6. [Raising Children Differently](06%20-%20Raising%20Children%20Differently.md) — the essay's closing case for one standard instead of two
