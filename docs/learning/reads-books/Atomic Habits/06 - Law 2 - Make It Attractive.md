# 06 — Law 2: Make It Attractive

<small>2 min read</small>

## Core idea
The second law targets the craving stage. Its main technique is **temptation bundling**: pairing an action you need to do with an action you want to do, so the craving for the second attaches itself to the first. Clear also argues craving is shaped heavily by the culture and people around you — we imitate the habits of three groups especially: the close (family, friends), the many (the crowd, social norms), and the powerful (people with status we want). Joining a group where your desired behavior is the normal behavior makes that behavior attractive almost automatically, because it becomes a form of belonging, not just self-discipline.

## Why it matters
Willpower to force yourself through an unattractive task is a depletable, unreliable resource. Making the behavior itself genuinely more attractive — or borrowing attraction from something already attractive — removes the need for willpower on a given day rather than trying to strengthen it. The social dimension matters because most habit advice treats habits as an individual, private struggle, when in practice the environment of people around you is one of the strongest predictors of whether a habit sticks — it's far easier to become a runner if you join a group of runners than to become one in isolation through sheer resolve.

## Example from the book
Clear's specific temptation-bundling example: a case where an engineer named Ronan Byrne wired his exercise bike to his TV's Netflix account, so the show would only keep playing while he kept pedaling — he could only watch Netflix while exercising. He also cites research on how joining "The Cult of CrossFit" or similar tight-knit fitness communities produces higher habit adherence than working out alone, not because the exercise itself is different, but because showing up becomes socially reinforced rather than a private negotiation with yourself.

## Practical application
Design your own temptation bundle using the formula: "After [habit I need], I will [something I want]" — for example, only allowing yourself a favorite podcast during a walk you're trying to establish, or only checking social media while on the treadmill. Separately, identify one group, community, or even a single accountability partner where the habit you want is already the norm, and find a way to join or borrow that normalcy.

## Something to sit with
> [!question]- A question to think about
> Think of a habit you find genuinely unattractive to start. Is there something you already crave that could be bundled with it — and separately, is there a group of people for whom this habit is simply "what we do" that you could join?
