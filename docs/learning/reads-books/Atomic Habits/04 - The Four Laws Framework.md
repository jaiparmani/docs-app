# 04 — The Four Laws Framework

<small>2 min read</small>

## Core idea
The book's operative framework, and the spine the rest of it is built on: to build a good habit, make it **obvious, attractive, easy,** and **satisfying**. Each law targets one stage of the habit loop from chunk 01 — obvious targets the cue, attractive targets the craving, easy targets the response, satisfying targets the reward. To break a bad habit, you invert all four: make it invisible, unattractive, difficult, and unsatisfying. This one framework is the organizing structure for essentially every tactic in the rest of the book — each subsequent law-chapter is really a toolbox of specific techniques for satisfying one of the four.

## Why it matters
Before this framework, habit advice tends to be a grab-bag of unrelated tips (drink water first thing, keep a journal, use a habit tracker) with no obvious relationship between them. The Four Laws give every tactic a "slot" — you can ask of any technique, "which of the four is this actually doing?" That turns habit design from folk wisdom into something closer to engineering: if a habit isn't sticking, you can diagnose which of the four laws is unmet, rather than concluding you simply lack discipline.

## Example from the book
Clear frames the whole book as an answer to a question he says most people never explicitly ask: why do we do what we do, over and over, often against our own stated goals? He argues nearly every habit failure can be traced to a violation of one of the four laws — a habit that fails to form because its cue was never made obvious (you own running shoes but they're in the back of the closet), or because it wasn't attractive (you associate the gym with discomfort, not anticipation), or because it wasn't easy (your habit requires 45 minutes and specialized equipment), or because it wasn't satisfying (the payoff is months away, not immediate).

## Practical application
For a habit you're currently trying to build, run it through all four laws as a checklist, in order: Is the cue for this habit obvious in my environment right now? Is the craving actually attractive, or am I gritting my teeth through it? Is the response as easy as I can currently make it? Does something about doing it feel satisfying in the moment, not just in six months? Whichever question you answer "no" to first is where your actual leverage is — not more willpower, but redesigning that specific stage.

## Something to sit with
> [!question]- A question to think about
> Take a habit that's never stuck no matter how many times you've tried it. Running it through the four questions above — which law is it actually failing on, and had you been trying to fix a different one instead?
