# The Psychology of Money

*Morgan Housel*

Housel's real claim is narrower than the title suggests: doing well with money has far less to do with what you know about finance than with how you behave — your patience, your fear, your ego, and your tolerance for being wrong. Broken into 12 concepts, one per chunk, each a 3-5 minute read. Each chunk covers the core idea, why it matters, a specific example from the book, a practical way to apply it, and a question to sit with. Read them in order — later chunks assume the earlier ones.

## Concepts

1. [No One's Crazy](01%20-%20No%20One's%20Crazy.md) — why other people's financial decisions make perfect sense to them
2. [Luck & Risk](02%20-%20Luck%20%26%20Risk.md) — why the people you study are never a full sample of the outcomes that were possible
3. [Never Enough](03%20-%20Never%20Enough.md) — the specific danger of moving your own goalposts
4. [Confounding Compounding](04%20-%20Confounding%20Compounding.md) — why almost all of Buffett's fortune arrived after he was old
5. [Getting Wealthy vs. Staying Wealthy](05%20-%20Getting%20Wealthy%20vs%20Staying%20Wealthy.md) — two different skills, and why only one of them compounds
6. [Tails, You Win](06%20-%20Tails%2C%20You%20Win.md) — how a small number of decisions account for almost all the results
7. [Freedom](07%20-%20Freedom.md) — the highest dividend money pays is control over your own time
8. [Man in the Car Paradox](08%20-%20Man%20in%20the%20Car%20Paradox.md) — why buying respect with money quietly fails at its own goal
9. [Wealth Is What You Don't See](09%20-%20Wealth%20Is%20What%20You%20Don't%20See.md) — spending is the thing that hides wealth, not the thing that shows it
10. [Save Money](10%20-%20Save%20Money.md) — why your savings rate matters more than your income or your returns
11. [Reasonable Over Rational](11%20-%20Reasonable%20Over%20Rational.md) — optimizing for the plan you can actually stick to, not the one that's mathematically perfect
12. [Room for Error](12%20-%20Room%20for%20Error.md) — planning for a range of outcomes instead of the one you expect
