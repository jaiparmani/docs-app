# Nudge
*Richard Thaler and Cass Sunstein*

Broken into 8 concepts, one per chunk, each a 3-5 minute read. Each chunk covers the core idea, why it matters, a specific example from the book, a practical way to apply it, and a question to sit with. Read them in order — later chunks build on the earlier ones.

## Concepts

1. [Humans vs. Econs](01%20-%20Humans%20vs.%20Econs.md) — the fictional rational optimizer economics assumes, and the messier real people institutions actually serve
2. [Biases and Blunders](02%20-%20Biases%20and%20Blunders.md) — the predictable, systematic ways judgment departs from the Econ ideal
3. [The Planner and the Doer](03%20-%20The%20Planner%20and%20the%20Doer.md) — why willpower fails structurally, not morally
4. [Choice Architecture](04%20-%20Choice%20Architecture.md) — why there's no neutral way to present a set of options
5. [Defaults](05%20-%20Defaults.md) — why the option nobody actively picks is often the one everybody ends up with
6. [Feedback and Mappings](06%20-%20Feedback%20and%20Mappings.md) — helping people predict, and later see, what a choice actually leads to
7. [Save More Tomorrow](07%20-%20Save%20More%20Tomorrow.md) — a real program that sidesteps loss aversion by saving out of future raises
8. [Libertarian Paternalism](08%20-%20Libertarian%20Paternalism.md) — the book's core claim: steering people while keeping the exit door easy isn't a contradiction
