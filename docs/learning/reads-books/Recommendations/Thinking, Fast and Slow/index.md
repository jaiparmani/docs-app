# Thinking, Fast and Slow
*Daniel Kahneman*

Kahneman's central claim: two systems run your mind — System 1, fast and intuitive, and System 2, slow and deliberate — and most of the errors in human judgment come from System 1 quietly answering questions System 2 was supposed to handle. Broken into 14 concepts, one per chunk, each a 3-5 minute read. Each chunk covers the core idea, why it matters, a specific example from the book, a practical way to apply it, and a question to sit with. Read them in order — the later biases build on the System 1 / System 2 framing from the first two chunks.

## Concepts

1. [System 1 and System 2](01%20-%20System%201%20and%20System%202.md) — the two-system model the whole book runs on
2. [The Lazy Controller](02%20-%20The%20Lazy%20Controller.md) — why System 2 defaults to rubber-stamping System 1's answers
3. [WYSIATI](03%20-%20WYSIATI.md) — "what you see is all there is," and why it makes overconfidence feel natural
4. [Anchoring](04%20-%20Anchoring.md) — how an irrelevant number quietly pulls your estimate toward it
5. [The Availability Heuristic](05%20-%20The%20Availability%20Heuristic.md) — judging frequency by how easily examples come to mind
6. [Representativeness and the Linda Problem](06%20-%20Representativeness%20and%20the%20Linda%20Problem.md) — when a good story beats a correct probability
7. [Regression to the Mean](07%20-%20Regression%20to%20the%20Mean.md) — the statistical illusion that looks like cause and effect
8. [The Planning Fallacy](08%20-%20The%20Planning%20Fallacy.md) — why every plan assumes the best case
9. [Loss Aversion](09%20-%20Loss%20Aversion.md) — why losses hurt roughly twice as much as equivalent gains feel good
10. [Prospect Theory and the Fourfold Pattern](10%20-%20Prospect%20Theory%20and%20the%20Fourfold%20Pattern.md) — the theory that actually won Kahneman the Nobel
11. [The Endowment Effect](11%20-%20The%20Endowment%20Effect.md) — why owning something inflates what you'd sell it for
12. [Framing Effects](12%20-%20Framing%20Effects.md) — the same facts, a different decision, depending on the wording
13. [The Experiencing Self and the Remembering Self](13%20-%20The%20Experiencing%20Self%20and%20the%20Remembering%20Self.md) — two different "yous" that disagree about what counts as a good experience
14. [Priming and the Replication Crisis](14%20-%20Priming%20and%20the%20Replication%20Crisis.md) — where the book's own evidence base was later challenged
