# 03 — WYSIATI: What You See Is All There Is

<small>2 min read</small>

## Core idea
Kahneman's acronym for one of System 1's most consequential habits: it constructs the best possible story from whatever information is currently available, and it does this without any built-in check for how much information is actually missing. WYSIATI means System 1 doesn't pause to ask "what don't I know?" — it treats the evidence in front of it as though it were the complete picture, then generates a confident, coherent impression on that basis. The amount of evidence and its quality barely register; what matters to System 1 is whether the available evidence forms a good story.

## Why it matters
WYSIATI is the mechanism underneath overconfidence itself. It explains why people can form strong, confident opinions about a person, a company, or a situation from a single data point — one good interview answer, one bad first impression, one headline — without any felt sense that they're working from a thin slice of reality. It also explains why more information doesn't reliably make people more cautious in their judgments: since System 1 is already satisfied with a coherent story from limited data, adding more information often doesn't trigger a "wait, I should reconsider" response unless it directly contradicts the story already formed.

## Example from the book
Kahneman uses the halo effect as a direct application of WYSIATI: if you like a political candidate's smile and voice, you tend to also like their policies, even though you have no real basis for that inference — System 1 built a single coherent impression from the first thing it noticed and extended it to everything else, unbothered by the huge gap in actual evidence. He also describes how people confidently predict a company's future success from a single charismatic CEO interview, ignoring the enormous amount of relevant information — the market, the balance sheet, competitors — that simply isn't in front of them and therefore doesn't factor into the confident story System 1 tells.

## Practical application
Before locking in a strong impression — of a person, a plan, an investment, a candidate — explicitly ask "what information would change my mind that I don't currently have?" If you can't name anything, that's often a sign you've mistaken a small, coherent slice of evidence for the whole picture rather than actually weighed the evidence's completeness. This is a deliberate System 2 override of a habit System 1 will not perform on its own.

## Something to sit with
> [!question]- A question to think about
> Recall a strong first impression you formed of someone or something from very little evidence. Did you ever go back and ask what you didn't know at the time — and would that missing information have changed the impression?
