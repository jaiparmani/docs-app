# 03 — The Three Types of Leverage

<small>2 min read</small>

## Core idea
Leverage is whatever multiplies the output of your judgment without multiplying your hours. Naval's taxonomy names three kinds: labor (other people's effort, working for you), capital (money, working for you), and what he groups together as products and media — code and content that replicate at essentially zero marginal cost. The first two are old and require permission: labor means managing people and navigating their incentives and politics, capital means someone has to first trust you with money. Code and media are new, historically speaking, and they're permissionless — anyone with an internet connection can write software or publish something that then scales to a billion people without asking anyone's approval or paying anyone a wage for each additional copy.

## Why it matters
This reframes what to actually build your specific knowledge toward. Labor and capital leverage both route through other people's gatekeeping — a boss has to trust you to manage a team, an investor has to trust you with a fund. Code and media route around that gatekeeping entirely, which is why Naval considers them the more accessible form of leverage for anyone starting without existing capital or social credentials. It also explains a puzzle: why some individuals with no employees and no investors can still generate outsized returns relative to their hours worked. They're not skipping leverage, they're using a form of it that doesn't require anyone's permission to deploy.

## Example from the book
Naval's own move into angel investing and into building software products (and later into podcasting and writing that circulated widely without a publisher's gate) is presented as an illustration of stacking these forms of leverage deliberately: capital leverage from the investing, permissionless code-and-media leverage from products and public writing that kept working and compounding attention long after the initial effort of creating them was finished. The point he returns to repeatedly is that a single well-aimed piece of code or writing can keep paying out to an audience of any size, at no additional marginal cost, in a way a service business genuinely cannot.

## Practical application
For whatever specific knowledge you're building (see the previous chunk), ask which form of leverage is the most realistic entry point for you right now — and default toward code or media if you're starting with neither capital nor a track record that would let you direct labor. Concretely: could the thing you know how to do be captured once, in writing, software, video, or audio, such that it keeps being useful to people after you've stopped actively producing it? That's the test for whether you're using permissionless leverage or just working harder inside the old kind.

## Something to sit with

> [!question]- A question to think about
> Of the four forms of leverage — labor, capital, code, media — which one are you currently relying on most, and is it the one that actually fits how much capital, trust, or permission you have access to right now?
