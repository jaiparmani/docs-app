# 09 — Beyond Diabetes: Skin, Hormones, and Cravings

<small>2 min read</small>

## Core idea
Inchauspé's argument extends well past the territory usually associated with blood sugar — diabetes risk, weight — into effects she says show up in people whose blood panels are entirely normal: persistent cravings, energy crashes, skin issues like acne and premature aging, and hormonal symptoms including those associated with PCOS. Her mechanism for skin specifically is glycation — a real chemical process where excess circulating glucose binds to proteins like collagen, which can stiffen and damage the skin's structural proteins over time, contributing to how skin ages. For hormonal symptoms, her argument runs through insulin's downstream effects on other hormones, since chronically elevated insulin can influence androgen levels and ovarian function in ways connected to PCOS symptoms.

## Why it matters
This is the part of the book that gives non-diabetic readers the strongest reason to care about something they'd otherwise associate only with a distant diabetes risk decades away — cravings, fatigue, and skin are immediate, felt experiences rather than abstract future risk. If glucose spikes really do connect to those day-to-day complaints, then managing the curve becomes relevant to someone with no metabolic diagnosis at all, not just a preventive measure against a disease that might arrive later. That immediacy is a large part of the book's appeal and its persuasive power.

## Example from the book
Inchauspé connects glycation — glucose molecules attaching to collagen and elastin in skin — to visible skin aging, describing it as a slow, cumulative process where chronically higher glucose exposure over years contributes to stiffer, less elastic skin structure. She also describes case-style accounts of readers or clients whose skin, energy, or cravings improved after adopting the hacks, framing consistent glucose management as a lever for these symptoms distinct from diet changes aimed purely at weight.

## Practical application
If you experience persistent cravings, skin breakouts, or energy crashes without another clear explanation, it's reasonable to treat glucose-curve management as one variable worth testing — not because it's guaranteed to be the cause, but because the hacks (order, pairing, movement) are low-cost and low-risk to try regardless of whether the mechanism behind skin or hormonal symptoms turns out to be as central as Inchauspé argues. Track the symptom alongside the hacks for a few weeks rather than assuming a direct causal link from a single reading.
