# 08 — Hack 6: Put Clothes on Your Carbs

<small>2 min read</small>

## Core idea
Inchauspé's phrase for this hack is "put clothes on your carbs" — never eat a starch or sugar naked, meaning alone and unpaired; always eat it alongside fiber, fat, or protein, which she describes as clothing that slows the rate glucose reaches the bloodstream. Bread eaten with butter or alongside a meal containing protein and vegetables behaves differently in the body than the same bread eaten by itself on an empty stomach; rice eaten with vegetables and protein behaves differently than the same rice eaten plain. The carbohydrate content doesn't change — what changes is what surrounds it, which slows gastric emptying and blunts how quickly its glucose is absorbed.

## Why it matters
This hack functions as something like the unifying principle underneath several of the others — the food-order hack (chunk 3) and the glucose-gremlins hack (chunk 5) are both, in a sense, specific applications of "don't eat carbs naked": ordering vegetables and protein first means the carbs that follow are effectively already clothed by what's been digested ahead of them, and repositioning a sweet snack to follow a meal instead of standing alone does the same thing. Naming it as its own principle gives a simple mental test that can be applied to a food or meal Inchauspé hasn't specifically covered: is this carbohydrate alone, or is it paired with something that will slow it down.

## Example from the book
Inchauspé contrasts eating a slice of bread by itself — which produces a fast, high spike because there's nothing to slow the starch's breakdown into glucose — with eating the same slice of bread spread with butter or alongside eggs, where the added fat and protein measurably flatten the resulting curve despite the bread itself being identical. She extends the same logic to fruit: fruit eaten alone spikes faster than the same fruit eaten with a handful of nuts or as part of a meal, not because the fruit changed, but because it's no longer "naked."

## Practical application
Before eating any starch or sugar on its own — a piece of toast, a banana, a handful of crackers, a scoop of rice — ask whether it has "clothes" on: some fiber, fat, or protein alongside it. If it doesn't, the fix isn't to skip the food, it's to add something small — nut butter on the toast, a few nuts with the banana, cheese with the crackers — before eating it plain.

## Something to sit with
> [!question]- A question to think about
> Think of the last carbohydrate you ate completely on its own — no fat, fiber, or protein alongside it. What's the smallest, most convenient thing you could pair with it next time without changing what you're actually eating?
