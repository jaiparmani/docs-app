# 07 — Hack 5: Move After You Eat

<small>2 min read</small>

## Core idea
The fifth hack shifts from food to timing of movement: moving your body — walking, standing, light activity, not necessarily formal exercise — within roughly the first hour after eating helps flatten the glucose spike from that meal. The mechanism is that contracting muscles pull glucose out of the bloodstream to use as fuel through a pathway that operates independently of insulin, so movement during the window when glucose from a meal is entering the blood gives muscles a second route to clear it, on top of whatever insulin is doing. She's explicit that this doesn't require a workout — a ten-minute walk after a meal is the version she recommends, not a trip to the gym.

## Why it matters
This hack is notable for decoupling "exercise" from "glucose management" in a way that's much more sustainable day to day — most people can't or won't work out after every meal, but a short walk after dinner, or even just standing and doing dishes instead of sitting down immediately, is achievable most days. It also reframes movement's value in a way separate from calorie burn: a ten-minute walk burns relatively few calories, but its glucose-flattening effect is disproportionate to that small calorie cost, because it's about *when* the movement happens relative to the meal, not how much energy it expends.

## Example from the book
Inchauspé references research comparing post-meal glucose curves with and without a short walk immediately after eating, showing a measurably lower peak and a faster return to baseline in the walking condition even though the walk itself was brief and low-intensity — not a run or a gym session, just a walk. She frames the muscle-contraction mechanism (glucose uptake into working muscle that doesn't require insulin) as the reason timing matters more than intensity here: a long walk taken hours after a meal doesn't blunt that meal's spike the way a short walk taken during the absorption window does.

## Practical application
After a meal that's likely to spike — anything with a significant starch or sugar component — build in ten minutes of movement within the following hour: a walk around the block, taking a phone call standing up and pacing, or doing chores rather than sitting down immediately. This is a much lower bar than "exercise more" as a general goal; it's specifically about attaching a small amount of movement to the after-meal window rather than adding a workout to your day.

## Something to sit with
> [!question]- A question to think about
> Think of your last few meals. How many were followed by ten minutes of sitting still versus ten minutes of movement — and how easy would it realistically be to build a short walk into the hour after your next dinner?
