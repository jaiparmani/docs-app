# 06 — Hack 4: Savory Breakfast Over Sweet

<small>2 min read</small>

## Core idea
Inchauspé singles out breakfast as disproportionately important, not because it's "the most important meal of the day" in the generic sense that phrase usually means, but because of a specific claim: the glucose response to your first meal sets a pattern that influences how your body responds to food for the rest of the day. A sweet, low-protein, low-fiber breakfast — cereal, pastries, juice, sweetened yogurt — tends to produce a sharp early spike, and she argues that spike primes a worse glucose response to meals eaten later, even if those later meals are identical to what someone who started with a savory breakfast ate. A savory breakfast built around protein, fat, and vegetables (eggs, avocado, leftovers from dinner) instead sets a flatter baseline that carries forward.

## Why it matters
This matters because breakfast is, for most people, the single most sugar-forward meal of the day by cultural default — cereal, granola, pastries, and juice are marketed and normalized specifically as breakfast food in a way sweet lunches and dinners aren't. If breakfast's effect really does compound across the day the way she describes, it means the highest-leverage single meal to intervene on isn't necessarily the biggest or most indulgent meal of the day, but the smallest, most habitual one — the one most people give the least thought to because it's eaten on autopilot.

## Example from the book
Inchauspé describes the standard Western breakfast — cereal with milk, a croissant, orange juice — as often the single worst offender for glucose spikes in a typical day, worse even than dessert after dinner, because it combines refined carbohydrate and sugar with almost no fiber, protein, or fat to buffer it, eaten first thing after an overnight fast when the body is primed to absorb quickly. Her suggested replacement leans savory: eggs, cheese, leftover vegetables, or other combinations built around protein and fat rather than starting the day with what amounts to the sharpest possible spike.

## Practical application
Look at your typical breakfast and ask whether it's built primarily around sugar and refined carbohydrate (cereal, pastry, juice, sweetened coffee drinks) or around protein, fat, and vegetables. Shifting even one component — swapping juice for water, adding eggs or leftovers from last night's dinner instead of cereal — moves the first meal of the day toward the flatter end of the curve without requiring an entirely new morning routine.

## Something to sit with
> [!question]- A question to think about
> What does your actual breakfast look like on a normal weekday, and is it built around sugar or around protein and fat? If it's the former, what's the smallest single swap you could make tomorrow?
