# 04 — Hack 2: Vinegar Before You Eat

<small>2 min read</small>

## Core idea
The second hack is to have something acidic — specifically vinegar, diluted in water or used in a salad dressing — before a meal that contains starch or sugar. Inchauspé's claim is that acetic acid, the active compound in vinegar, interferes with the enzymes that break starch down into individual glucose molecules in the digestive tract, and separately improves how effectively muscle cells take up glucose once it does enter the bloodstream. The practical version is small: a tablespoon of vinegar in a large glass of water, drunk shortly before eating, or a vinegar-based dressing on a salad eaten at the start of the meal (which also folds in the food-order hack from chunk 3).

## Why it matters
Of all the hacks, this is the one that most resembles a "trick" rather than a structural change to how or what you eat, which is exactly why it's worth separating what's well supported from what's oversold. Vinegar's effect on post-meal glucose has genuine support from smaller clinical trials — it's a real, mechanistically plausible effect, not an invented one — but the effect size in that research is modest, and Inchauspé's prose sometimes reads with more confidence than a modest effect size earns. It matters less as a decisive lever and more as a nearly-free addition that stacks on top of the food-order hack rather than replacing it.

## Example from the book
Inchauspé cites research on vinegar consumed before a carbohydrate-containing meal producing a measurably lower post-meal glucose spike compared with the same meal eaten without vinegar, attributing the effect to acetic acid partially inhibiting the enzymes (like alpha-amylase) responsible for breaking starch into absorbable glucose. She frames it as one of the cheapest interventions in the book — apple cider vinegar and a glass of water cost close to nothing — which is consistent with her broader framing of these hacks as accessible rather than requiring special products.

## Practical application
Before a meal you know will include bread, pasta, rice, or something sweet, mix a tablespoon of vinegar (apple cider vinegar is the version she references most, though the acetic-acid mechanism isn't specific to that brand) into a large glass of water and drink it in the ten or so minutes before eating. If a vinegar-based salad dressing is already part of the meal, eating that salad first does double duty — it's both the vegetable-first sequencing from chunk 3 and the vinegar hack in one step.

## Something to sit with
> [!question]- A question to think about
> Of the specific numbered hacks in this book, vinegar is one of the ones with a real but modest evidence base. Does knowing the effect size is smaller than the book's confident tone suggests change whether you'd bother trying it?
