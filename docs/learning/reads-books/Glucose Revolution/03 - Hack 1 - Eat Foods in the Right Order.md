# 03 — Hack 1: Eat Foods in the Right Order

<small>2 min read</small>

## Core idea
The first and, per the book's own framing, most load-bearing hack is sequencing: within a single meal, eat vegetables first, then protein and fat, and save starches and sugars — bread, rice, pasta, dessert — for last. The food on the plate doesn't have to change at all; what changes is the order it enters the digestive system. Fiber from vegetables, eaten first, begins forming a mesh-like barrier in the gut before the carbohydrate arrives, and protein and fat eaten before carbohydrate slow gastric emptying, so that by the time starch and sugar hit the small intestine, glucose is absorbed more gradually rather than dumped in all at once.

## Why it matters
This is the hack Inchauspé treats as doing most of the practical work in the book, and it's notable for what it doesn't require: no food is eliminated, no calories are counted, no ingredient is swapped out. For anyone who has tried and abandoned restrictive diets, "eat the same meal, just in a different order" is a dramatically lower-friction change — it requires zero new grocery purchases and no new cooking skill, only a different sequence of bites at a meal you were already going to eat. That low friction is precisely why she positions it as hack number one: it's the easiest of all the interventions to actually sustain past the first week.

## Example from the book
Inchauspé points to research on food order and post-meal glucose response — studies giving the same meal (say, a sandwich, salad, and juice) to the same people on different days, varying only the order components were eaten in — showing that eating the vegetable and protein components before the bread and juice measurably blunts the glucose spike compared with eating in reverse order, or eating everything mixed together. The mechanism she cites is that fiber ahead of carbohydrate slows the rate glucose enters the bloodstream, turning a sharp spike into a more gradual rise — same food, same total sugar, meaningfully different curve.

## Practical application
At your next meal with more than one component, physically eat in this order: vegetables first, then protein and fat, then starches and anything sweet last — including dessert, which should come at the true end rather than being folded into the middle of the meal. This applies even to something as simple as a sandwich: eating the salad side dish before the sandwich, or eating around the bread and finishing it last, applies the same principle without needing to prepare anything differently.

## Something to sit with
> [!question]- A question to think about
> At your next meal, could you eat the exact same food, just in a different order — vegetables and protein first, starch last — without buying or preparing anything differently? What's stopping you from trying it once as an experiment?
