# 08 — Intermediate Packets

<small>2 min read</small>

## Core idea
An intermediate packet is any discrete, reusable unit of finished or semi-finished work — a distilled note, an outline, a rough draft, a set of curated quotes, a slide you built for one deck — that's small enough to stand on its own and valuable enough to reuse later, in a different project, for a different purpose. Forte's point is that most knowledge work output isn't actually built from scratch each time, even though it often gets approached that way; it's assembled from smaller pieces, many of which already exist somewhere in your notes if your Distill step has been doing its job.

## Why it matters
Treating every new project as a blank page is one of the most expensive habits in knowledge work — it means re-deriving research, re-writing explanations, and re-solving problems you've effectively already solved before, just because the earlier version lived inside a different project's folder. Intermediate packets reframe your second brain as a library of pre-made components rather than a single-use archive per project: a well-distilled note from one context can become the seed of an outline in a completely unrelated one. This is where PARA's actionability-based organization and Progressive Summarization's layered distillation actually pay off — a note that's already been condensed to its essence is far easier to lift out of its original context and reuse than raw, unprocessed material would be.

## Example from the book
Forte describes how a single well-distilled note or outline built for one project — a client presentation, say — can resurface almost unchanged as the backbone of an entirely different piece of work months later, whether that's a blog post, a different client's presentation, or a section of a book. The packet doesn't need to be reinvented; it needs to be found, which is precisely what an actionability-based, distilled system makes possible in a way a topic-based pile of raw notes doesn't.

## Practical application
Before starting your next piece of work, spend five minutes searching your own notes for anything you've already captured, distilled, or drafted that's even loosely related — a past outline, a relevant quote you saved, a summary you wrote for something else. Treat anything usable as a head start rather than starting from a blank page. Over time, also get in the habit of saving the small byproducts of your current work — an outline, a research summary — as their own standalone notes, specifically so they're findable as intermediate packets later.

## Something to sit with
> [!question]- A question to think about
> Think of the last project you built entirely from scratch. Is there a good chance you'd already solved part of that problem before, in a different context — and if so, why didn't that earlier work surface when you needed it?
