# 04 — The Twelve Favorite Problems

<small>2 min read</small>

## Core idea
"Keep what resonates" is a good filter, but Forte pairs it with a sharper technique borrowed from physicist Richard Feynman, who reportedly kept a running list of about a dozen favorite open problems in his head at all times. Whenever he encountered a new fact, trick, or result in any field, he'd test it against each of his twelve problems to see if it offered a new angle of attack. Forte adapts this into a note-taking practice: write down a list of the open questions you're genuinely curious about — phrased as questions, not yes/no facts — and let that list actively direct what you capture as you move through the world.

## Why it matters
Resonance alone can feel directionless — you save what strikes you, but strikes you toward what end? The Twelve Favorite Problems gives capture a compass without turning it into rigid, narrow research. Because the problems are phrased as open questions ("how can my industry become more sustainable?" rather than "should I switch industries?"), they don't foreclose answers in advance — they just make you more likely to notice when something you encounter is quietly relevant to a question you actually care about, even in an unrelated context. It also makes captured material easier to Organize and Express later, because notes taken with a favorite problem in mind already have a purpose attached, instead of needing one assigned retroactively.

## Example from the book
Forte recounts Feynman's own account of the practice: by holding a fixed set of problems constantly in mind, Feynman would notice when a new technique from an entirely different area of physics turned out to solve one of his twelve, producing insights that looked like luck from the outside but were really the product of a sustained, deliberate filter running in the background at all times. Forte's adaptation asks readers to write their own list — a mix of professional and personal questions — and revisit and revise it periodically, since the problems you care about shift as your life and work change.

## Practical application
Write down somewhere visible five to twelve questions you're genuinely curious about right now — they can span work, relationships, health, or anything else, as long as they're open-ended and you actually care about the answer. Over the next week, notice how often something you read, hear, or observe turns out to bear on one of them, even in a context that has nothing to do with where the question came from. That noticing is the mechanism the technique is actually built on.

## Something to sit with
> [!question]- A question to think about
> If you had to name your own twelve favorite problems right now, how many could you list without stopping to think — and what does the length of that pause tell you about how deliberately you're currently directing your attention?
