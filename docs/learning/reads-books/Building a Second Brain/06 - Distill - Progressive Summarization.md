# 06 — Distill — Progressive Summarization

<small>2 min read</small>

## Core idea
Progressive Summarization is Forte's technique for the Distill step: instead of summarizing a note once and calling it done, you condense it in layers, applied over multiple separate visits rather than all at once. Layer one is the raw, saved note. Layer two is bolding the passages that stand out most on a re-read. Layer three is highlighting the best of what you already bolded — the best of the best. Layer four, used sparingly for your most valuable notes, is a short executive summary written in your own words at the top. Each layer uses different formatting, so a later reader — usually future you — can tell at a glance how heavily a note has already been processed.

## Why it matters
A note captured once and never revisited tends to be nearly as opaque to future-you as it was to a stranger — you saved it because it resonated in the moment, but that context fades fast. Summarizing everything to the same depth immediately is also its own trap: most captured notes never get reused, so front-loading heavy summarization work onto all of them wastes effort on material that was never going to matter. Progressive Summarization solves both problems by making distillation lazy and incremental — you only invest a second or third layer of effort into a note when you've actually returned to it, which is itself evidence the note is worth the investment.

## Example from the book
Forte describes returning to a highlighted book note weeks or months later and bolding only the highlights that still stand out on a second read — often a small fraction of the original highlights. On a further pass, perhaps while researching a specific project, he highlights the best of those bolded passages, and occasionally, for a note he ends up returning to repeatedly, adds a one-line summary at the very top so that the next time he opens it, he doesn't have to re-read any of the layers below to get the gist.

## Practical application
Pick an old note you've saved but haven't touched since capturing it — ideally one you're about to use for something. Instead of rereading it in full, skim only the parts you've already marked as notable (if none, this is layer one). Bold the two or three passages that still jump out. That's the entire technique in miniature: each visit adds one layer, and you stop as soon as the note is usable for what you currently need it for, not before.

## Something to sit with
> [!question]- A question to think about
> Think of a note you've reread multiple times without ever condensing it further. What's stopped you from bolding or summarizing it — and would doing so on your next visit actually cost you more than rereading the whole thing does?
