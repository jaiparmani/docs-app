# 03 — Capture — Keep What Resonates

<small>2 min read</small>

## Core idea
Capture is the first CODE step, and Forte's criterion for it is deliberately loose: keep what resonates. Not "keep everything," which produces an unusable dump, and not "keep only what you're certain you'll need," which is nearly impossible to judge in advance. Resonance is a feeling — a quote that stops you, a fact that surprises you, an idea that connects to something you're already chewing on — and Forte's bet is that this feeling is a more reliable filter than any rule-based checklist, because it's tracking something your conscious mind hasn't fully articulated yet.

## Why it matters
The instinct most people bring to note-taking is completeness — capture everything from a book, a meeting, an article, "just in case." Forte argues this is close to the opposite of useful: a system where everything is saved is a system where nothing is findable, and the effort spent capturing indiscriminately is effort not spent on the steps that actually produce output. Trusting resonance instead of completeness makes capture fast and sustainable, because you're not trying to be a comprehensive archive of everything you've ever encountered — you're building a personal, opinionated collection of the material that has already proven itself relevant to you.

## Example from the book
Forte contrasts two ways of taking notes on a book: transcribing or highlighting large sections start to finish, versus stopping only at the handful of passages that produce an actual reaction — surprise, disagreement, a connection to something else you're working on. He points out that months later, when you return to a note, the sections you resonated with are the ones you'll actually reread and use; the exhaustively transcribed material is the part you skip past, because it was never filtered by anything except "this was in the book," which isn't a signal of future usefulness at all.

## Practical application
Next time you're reading or in a conversation and you feel that small internal pull — "that's interesting," "I disagree with that," "that connects to X" — treat the feeling itself as the capture trigger, not a mental note to "write it down properly later." Capture it immediately, briefly, in whatever tool you actually keep open, even if it's just a sentence. The goal isn't a polished note yet — Distill happens later — the goal at this stage is just not losing the moment the resonance happened.

## Something to sit with
> [!question]- A question to think about
> Look at the last few things you saved or highlighted. Did you save them because they genuinely resonated, or out of a vague sense that you should? What would your capture habits look like if you only kept the first kind?
