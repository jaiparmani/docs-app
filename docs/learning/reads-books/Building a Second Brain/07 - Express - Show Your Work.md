# 07 — Express — Show Your Work

<small>2 min read</small>

## Core idea
Express is the fourth CODE step and, in Forte's own framing, the whole point of the other three — capture, organize, and distill only pay off when captured material actually becomes something: a document, a decision, a piece of shared work. Forte's central push in this chapter is against a specific bad habit: waiting until an idea, or a body of research, feels complete and polished before sharing or using any of it. He argues for expressing earlier, more often, and in smaller pieces than feels natural — treating output as a continuous practice rather than a single high-stakes event that only happens once everything is "ready."

## Why it matters
Capture, organize, and distill can all be done alone, privately, with no risk of being judged — which is exactly why they're comfortable to over-invest in. Express is the step that exposes your thinking to other people, or to your own scrutiny once it's written down in finished form, and that discomfort is a large part of why so many well-organized note systems never produce much visible output. Forte's insistence on expressing early and in small pieces is a direct countermeasure: smaller, more frequent output lowers the stakes of any single piece of work, gets you feedback while an idea is still cheap to change, and builds the habit of finishing rather than only accumulating.

## Example from the book
Forte contrasts the writer who sits on a single massive project for months, terrified to show anyone an unfinished draft, with the practice of sharing smaller units of work along the way — a rough outline, a partial draft, a single distilled note repurposed into a short piece of writing. The smaller unit gets feedback faster, gets finished more often, and — because it drew on material already captured, organized, and distilled — takes a fraction of the time a from-scratch equivalent would, since none of the underlying thinking has to be re-derived.

## Practical application
Take the smallest complete piece of output you could realistically produce today from material you've already captured — a single paragraph, a short answer to a question someone asked you, a one-page outline instead of a full draft — and finish and share it before moving on to anything larger. The goal isn't ambition-lowering; it's building the muscle of turning stored material into finished output on a short cycle, rather than only on the rare occasion an entire project comes together at once.

## Something to sit with
> [!question]- A question to think about
> What's a piece of work you've been sitting on because it doesn't feel finished enough to show anyone yet? What's the smallest honest fragment of it you could share today instead?
