# 07 — The Two Coffee Method: Front-Loading the Hard Work

<small>3 min read</small>

## Core idea
This tactic pairs the book's Laser and Energize ideas together: use the early part of the day — roughly the stretch covered by your first one or two cups of coffee — to go straight into your Highlight before opening email, Slack, or any other inbox. The "two coffee" framing is a simple, memorable marker for a specific window: the first coffee gets you alert and starting, and the second is treated as the signal to stay on the same task rather than as a cue to switch into checking messages, so the whole early window stays committed to the one thing you chose rather than fragmenting after the first interruption.

## Why it matters
Willpower and focus aren't flat across the day — they're highest early, before the accumulated friction of meetings, requests, and minor decisions has worn them down. Most people spend exactly that high-energy window on the lowest-value task available: clearing an inbox, which is really just processing other people's priorities. By the time the inbox is "handled," the energy that could have gone toward the Highlight has already been spent on everyone else's agenda, and what's left for the actual priority is the tired, depleted version of your attention. Front-loading the hard, chosen work into the window before you even open email flips that order, so the highest-value task gets the highest-quality attention instead of the leftovers. This tactic is also a good example of how the book links its four steps together in practice — it's simultaneously a Laser tactic (avoiding the inbox as a distraction) and an Energize tactic (matching the hardest work to the period of highest natural alertness), which is typical of how the individual tactics in the book's menu often serve more than one step at once.

## Example from the book
The authors describe their own practice of protecting that early stretch specifically from email and messaging — not glancing at either until the Highlight work is genuinely underway or done — treating the caffeine ritual itself as a small, repeatable anchor that marks "this time belongs to the Highlight" rather than "this time belongs to whatever just came in." It's presented less as a rigid rule than as one of the book's many small, testable tactics: a way of using an existing daily ritual (coffee) as a trigger for a behavior you want (sustained focus) instead of letting it become an incidental pause before checking a phone.

## Practical application
Tomorrow, don't open email, Slack, or any messaging app until your Highlight work is underway — use your coffee (or equivalent morning ritual) as the deliberate marker for "focus time starts now" rather than "time to check in." If a second cup is part of your routine, treat it as permission to keep going on the same task rather than as a natural break point for switching to messages.

## Something to sit with
> [!question]- A question to think about
> On a typical day, what's the very first thing you do with your attention after you're fully awake — and is it something you chose, or something someone else's message chose for you?
