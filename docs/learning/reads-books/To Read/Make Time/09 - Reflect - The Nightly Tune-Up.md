# 09 — Reflect: The Nightly Tune-Up

<small>3 min read</small>

## Core idea
The fourth and final daily step is a short evening review: did the Highlight happen, which Laser and Energize tactics were actually used, what helped, what didn't, and what's worth trying differently tomorrow. Reflect is what keeps the whole system from being a fixed program you either follow perfectly or fail at — it turns each day into a small, self-contained experiment whose results directly inform the next one.

## Why it matters
Knapp and Zeratsky are explicit that the book is a menu of roughly 80-plus tactics, not a single prescribed system everyone should run identically — and Reflect is the mechanism that makes that menu structure actually work in practice. Without a nightly check-in, tactics either get adopted permanently on a whim or abandoned permanently after one bad day, neither of which is a real evaluation. With Reflect, each tactic gets a fair, repeated trial and an honest verdict based on your own results, which is what lets the system become genuinely personalized over weeks rather than staying a generic list you tried once. It also does something subtler: it separates the question "did I fail today" from the question "did this specific tactic work," which matters because the two get conflated constantly. A missed Highlight isn't proof the whole system is broken — it might just mean one tactic needs swapping out, and Reflect is the moment that distinction gets made instead of assumed.

## Example from the book
The authors describe keeping the reflection genuinely brief — a few lines at the end of the day, not a journaling practice — noting simply whether the Highlight got done, what specific tactic (a blocked site, a distraction-free phone, an earlier bedtime) seemed to help or not, and one thing to adjust tomorrow. The emphasis throughout is on treating the whole book as something you test on yourself rather than something you follow on faith: a tactic that doesn't earn its place after a fair trial gets dropped without guilt, and one that does gets kept. This is consistent with the design-sprint mindset the authors bring from their Google Ventures background — a short cycle of build, test, and learn, run on your own daily habits instead of a product, with the nightly review standing in for the "test" phase that tells you whether to keep iterating on a tactic or move on to the next one on the menu.

## Practical application
Tonight, write three short lines: did today's Highlight happen, what tactic (if any) you used and whether it helped, and one thing to try or change tomorrow. Keep it to under a minute — the value is in the daily habit of honest, brief review, not in the depth of any single entry. If a tactic has had a fair trial of several days and clearly isn't helping, let Reflect give you permission to drop it rather than treating persistence with a tactic that isn't working as a virtue in itself.

## Something to sit with
> [!question]- A question to think about
> If you'd kept a two-line nightly log for the past month, what pattern do you suspect it would have shown about which days your Highlight actually got done — and which days it quietly didn't?
