# 02 — Choosing Your Highlight: Urgency, Satisfaction, or Joy

<small>3 min read</small>

## Core idea
Picking the Highlight isn't left to instinct — Knapp and Zeratsky give three deliberate lenses for the morning decision of what one thing to prioritize: **Urgency** (what's demanding attention today, a deadline or a commitment that can't slide), **Satisfaction** (what will leave you with the strongest sense of having accomplished something, even if nothing forces you to do it today), and **Joy** (what would you most enjoy doing, independent of obligation or output). The suggestion isn't to run all three every day; it's to ask which lens fits today and let that answer name the Highlight, so the choice takes under a minute rather than becoming its own source of procrastination.

## Why it matters
Without a deliberate choice, the default "highlight" of most days is whatever shows up loudest first — the first Slack message, the first email, the first meeting invite — which means the day's shape gets set by other people's priorities before you've set any of your own. The three-lens question exists specifically to interrupt that default. It also solves a subtler problem: a Highlight chosen purely on urgency, day after day, quietly turns your life into an inbox you're perpetually clearing, while one chosen purely on joy, day after day, risks drifting from actual obligations. Rotating the lens by what a given day actually calls for is the book's answer to keeping the practice sustainable rather than becoming either pure firefighting or pure indulgence.

## Example from the book
The authors describe using this as a genuinely quick morning routine — often done with a notebook or a sticky note before looking at a screen at all — where you ask the three questions in order and stop as soon as one produces a clear answer: is there something urgent today that has to happen? If not, what would be most satisfying to have finished by tonight? If neither is obvious, what would bring the most joy? The Highlight itself is meant to be modest in scope — something achievable in roughly 60 to 90 minutes of focused time, not a multi-day project — because the point is a single, completable unit you can actually finish and feel finished by, not an open-ended aspiration.

## Practical application
Tomorrow morning, before opening your phone, write down the three questions and answer them in order, stopping at the first clear answer. Resist the pull to pick something huge — if your honest Highlight is a large project, carve out just the next concrete 60-90 minute piece of it rather than naming the whole project. Notice, over a week, which lens you reach for most often; if it's always Urgency, that's worth noticing on its own, since it suggests your days are currently being run entirely by other people's deadlines.

## Something to sit with
> [!question]- A question to think about
> Think back over the last week. If you'd asked the Urgency/Satisfaction/Joy question each morning, would any of your actual days have looked different — or has "urgent" quietly been your only lens for so long that the other two barely register as options anymore?
