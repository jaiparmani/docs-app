# 07 — Own Thy Home, Insure Thy Income, Increase Thy Earning

<small>3 min read</small>

## Core idea
The last three cures are shorter in the book than the first four, but round out the system: make your dwelling a profitable investment rather than a pure expense, provide in advance for a future when your earning power will be reduced, and continually work to increase your own ability to earn. Owning your home converts what would otherwise be an ongoing cost — rent, paid to someone else, that never returns anything to you — into an asset that builds value while you live in it. Providing a future income means setting money and provisions aside for old age or for family before the need is urgent, since the years when you can no longer easily earn arrive whether or not you've prepared for them. Increasing your earning ability means treating your own skill and knowledge as something to deliberately invest in, not something fixed at whatever level you happened to start with.

## Why it matters
These three cures share a common thread: they all convert something you might otherwise treat as static — where you live, your future security, your earning capacity — into something you actively grow. A renter who never buys is paying indefinitely for an asset someone else owns; a saver who doesn't also insure a future income is assuming their current earning power will simply continue forever; and someone who never invests in their own skills is assuming their current wage is a ceiling rather than a starting point. Each is a place where passivity quietly costs money over a long enough timeline, even though none of them feel urgent day to day.

## Example from the book
Arkad points out that a man who rents his whole life pays out money continuously and has nothing to show for it afterward, whereas the same payments directed toward owning a home eventually leave him with a valuable asset and reduced future expenses — and often room to save even more once the home is his outright. For the future-income cure, Arkad frames old age as a certainty to plan for, not a risk to hope against, urging his students to set something aside for the years when they can no longer work as they once did, and for their families should misfortune arrive early. And on earning ability, Arkad's own biography is the proof: he didn't stay a scribe earning a scribe's wage — he deliberately built skill and reputation that let his earning capacity grow well beyond where he started.

## Practical application
Treat these three as a longer-horizon checklist alongside the first four cures: is your housing cost building you an asset or simply disappearing, do you have anything set aside for a future when your current income won't be available, and are you actively investing time or money into skills that raise your earning ceiling rather than assuming your current wage is permanent? None of these require dramatic action immediately — but each compounds, like the gold in the earlier cures, the earlier you start.

> [!question]- A question to think about
> Of housing, future security, and earning capacity, which one have you been treating as fixed that's actually something you could be deliberately growing?
