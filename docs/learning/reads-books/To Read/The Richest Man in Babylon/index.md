# The Richest Man in Babylon
*George S. Clason*

Originally a series of 1920s pamphlets handed out by banks and insurance companies, later collected into one volume. Broken into 10 concepts, one per chunk, each a 3-5 minute read, teaching personal finance through parables set in ancient Babylon rather than direct argument. Read them in order — the later parables assume the earlier cures.

## Concepts

1. [Arkad and the Richest Man in Babylon](01%20-%20Arkad%20and%20the%20Richest%20Man%20in%20Babylon.md) — how a poor scribe became the wealthiest man in the city, and the one line that started it
2. [The Seven Cures for a Lean Purse](02%20-%20The%20Seven%20Cures%20for%20a%20Lean%20Purse.md) — the organizing framework the rest of the book hangs on
3. [Pay Yourself First](03%20-%20Pay%20Yourself%20First.md) — save a tenth before anything else touches your income
4. [Control Thy Expenditures](04%20-%20Control%20Thy%20Expenditures.md) — the honest line between needs and habits you've never questioned
5. [Make Thy Gold Multiply](05%20-%20Make%20Thy%20Gold%20Multiply.md) — putting savings to work so they earn on their own
6. [Guard Thy Treasures From Loss](06%20-%20Guard%20Thy%20Treasures%20From%20Loss.md) — protecting principal, and why expertise matters more than confidence
7. [Own Thy Home, Insure Thy Income, Increase Thy Earning](07%20-%20Own%20Thy%20Home%2C%20Insure%20Thy%20Income%2C%20Increase%20Thy%20Earning.md) — the three longer-horizon cures rounding out the system
8. [The Five Laws of Gold](08%20-%20The%20Five%20Laws%20of%20Gold.md) — Arkad's son Nomasir, sent out to lose and then relearn the lessons himself
9. [Dabasir and the Debt](09%20-%20Dabasir%20and%20the%20Debt.md) — the camel trader who fled his creditors and found his way back through a sustained budget
10. [Simple, Timeless, and Thin](10%20-%20Simple%2C%20Timeless%2C%20and%20Thin.md) — where this holds up, where it's thin, and the contrast with Housel's more evidence-driven approach
