# 02 — The Seven Cures for a Lean Purse

<small>3 min read</small>

## Core idea
This is the organizing framework the rest of the book hangs on, the way the Four Laws structure Atomic Habits. Later in the story, the King of Babylon summons Arkad after learning that he alone holds most of the city's gold, and asks him why so few men can acquire all the wealth while so many struggle. Arkad's answer is that wealth follows knowledge of certain laws, and those laws can be taught. The king asks him to teach a class of a hundred men over seven days, one lesson per day, so they can go on to teach others. The seven lessons became known as the Seven Cures for a Lean Purse: start thy purse to fattening, control thy expenditures, make thy gold multiply, guard thy treasures from loss, make of thy dwelling a profitable investment, ensure a future income, and increase thy ability to earn.

## Why it matters
Like the Four Laws in Atomic Habits, the Seven Cures turn scattered financial advice into a checklist with an internal order. Each cure builds on the one before it: you can't multiply gold you never started saving, and you can't guard treasure you haven't yet accumulated. The framework also reframes a "lean purse" as a diagnosable condition rather than bad luck — if your finances aren't improving, you can ask which of the seven you're actually failing to practice, instead of concluding you simply don't earn enough. That diagnostic use is the real value of naming them as a numbered sequence rather than a loose pile of tips.

## Example from the book
Arkad structures his own teaching this way deliberately — the king wants results for the whole city, not just for the hundred men in the room, so Arkad gives them something transferable: a short, ordered, memorable list rather than a sprawling philosophy. Each of the hundred men in the class is expected to leave able to both practice and explain the cures to others, which is why the book frames them as numbered "cures" for a specific ailment (a purse that stays lean no matter how much flows through it) rather than as abstract virtues.

## Practical application
Treat the seven cures as a sequence to audit yourself against, in order, rather than picking whichever one sounds appealing this month. Start with the first: are you actually setting aside a fixed portion of every payment before anything else touches it? If not, nothing downstream — multiplying gold, guarding it, investing in a home — has anything to work with yet. The individual chunks that follow in this collection each unpack one cure in more depth, but the framework itself is the thing worth internalizing first: a lean purse has a specific, ordered set of causes, and you can locate exactly where yours breaks down.

> [!question]- A question to think about
> If you ran your own finances through these seven cures in order, which one would you hit first — the one where the chain actually breaks?
