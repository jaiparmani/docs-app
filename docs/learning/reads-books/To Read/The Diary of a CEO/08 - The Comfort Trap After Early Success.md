# 08 — The Comfort Trap After Early Success

<small>2 min read</small>

## Core idea
The more dangerous threat to a growing venture often isn't failure — it's comfort arriving too early, once initial success removes the pressure that produced the original hustle, discipline, and hunger in the first place. Bartlett treats success itself as a risk factor that has to be actively managed, not just a reward that's safe to enjoy.

## Why it matters
This reframes "success" as something that changes your incentives in ways that aren't automatically good. The edge that got someone to their first real win — financial pressure, something to prove, nothing much to lose — very often dulls precisely at the moment it starts paying off, because the conditions that produced it have been removed by the win. The law argues for deliberately preserving some of the constraints or discomforts that produced results in the first place, rather than assuming that more resources and more safety are strictly beneficial. It's also a useful corrective to survivorship narratives that treat an early win as the end of the hard part, when the book's argument is closer to the opposite: the early win is what quietly starts the clock on the next, less visible risk.

## Example from the book
Bartlett describes watching this pattern repeatedly in the founders he's interviewed, and, by his own account, in himself: the moment a business starts generating real revenue or real attention is often the exact moment the original edge starts to dull, because the stakes that sharpened it have quietly disappeared. He argues that the people and businesses that keep growing past that point are the ones who notice the dulling as it happens and consciously reintroduce stakes — a harder goal, a public commitment, a real deadline — rather than coasting on the comfort the initial win purchased.

## Practical application
Periodically ask whether your current effort is being driven by genuine hunger or simply by momentum from a past win. If it's the latter, look for a way to reintroduce real stakes deliberately — a public commitment, a harder target, an actual deadline with consequences — rather than assuming the comfort you've accumulated is safe to keep indefinitely. Treat any stretch where nothing feels difficult as a prompt to check, not a reward to relax into.

> [!question]- A question to think about
> What decision have you made recently that you'd only make if you'd already stopped being hungry?
