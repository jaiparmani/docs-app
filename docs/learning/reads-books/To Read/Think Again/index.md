# Think Again
*Adam Grant*

Grant's case that rethinking — treating your own opinions as hypotheses to test rather than positions to defend — is an underrated skill that tends to get worse, not better, with expertise. It's a natural follow-up to *Originals*: where that book was about generating and championing novel ideas, this one applies the same underlying bet — psychologically flexible thinking beats rigid conviction — specifically to the harder, more personal skill of admitting you were wrong. Broken into 9 concepts, one per chunk, each a 3-5 minute read; read them roughly in order, since later chunks build on the mindset framework the first chunk sets up.

## Concepts

1. [The Four Mindsets](01%20-%20The%20Four%20Mindsets.md) — preacher, prosecutor, politician, and the scientist mode that actually updates
2. [Confident Humility](02%20-%20Confident%20Humility.md) — the sweet spot between the armchair quarterback and the impostor
3. [Why Expertise Breeds Overconfidence](03%20-%20Why%20Expertise%20Breeds%20Overconfidence.md) — Samuel Langley, the Wright brothers, and the cost of a defended position
4. [Task Conflict vs Relationship Conflict](04%20-%20Task%20Conflict%20vs%20Relationship%20Conflict.md) — arguing like you're right, listening like you're wrong
5. [Rethinking Under Pressure](05%20-%20Rethinking%20Under%20Pressure.md) — why crisis makes it hardest to let go of a plan that's stopped working
6. [The Binary Bias](06%20-%20The%20Binary%20Bias.md) — how flattening an issue into two sides makes rethinking feel like defection
7. [Teaching Rethinking in Classrooms and Workplaces](07%20-%20Teaching%20Rethinking%20in%20Classrooms%20and%20Workplaces.md) — building cultures that reward a changed mind instead of punishing it
8. [Psychological Safety](08%20-%20Psychological%20Safety.md) — the precondition that lets a group actually voice its doubts
9. [Helping Others Rethink](09%20-%20Helping%20Others%20Rethink.md) — motivational interviewing and why people persuade themselves better than you can persuade them
