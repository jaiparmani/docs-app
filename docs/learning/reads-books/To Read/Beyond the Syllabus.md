# Beyond the Syllabus

<small>3 min read</small>

*Ankur Warikoo*

Warikoo built his public profile on blunt, practical personal-finance and career content aimed at working adults — his earlier book *Do Epic Shit* and his social media presence are pitched at people already navigating jobs and money. *Beyond the Syllabus* is a deliberate pivot in audience rather than subject: same no-nonsense, direct-address style, but aimed specifically at teenagers, and specifically at the things school doesn't teach — confidence, basic money skills, how to handle relationships and peer pressure — packaged as the "real-life guide" the syllabus leaves out. The premise is straightforward and stated in the title: formal education covers marks, not the skills that actually determine whether a teenager navigates the next decade of their life well.

## What it's actually trying to do

The book's implicit claim is that a specific set of non-academic skills — emotional regulation, basic financial literacy, how to have hard conversations, how to handle failure and comparison — are teachable in the same direct, practical register Warikoo uses for adult career advice, just recalibrated for a teenage reader's actual concerns (grades, parents, friend groups, first jobs or allowances) rather than a working adult's (salary negotiation, investing, career pivots). That's a real gap: most teen-facing self-help either goes vague-and-inspirational or gets repackaged from adult content without genuinely adjusting the examples and stakes, and Warikoo's specific value-add, if the book lives up to his other work, is refusing to talk down to the reader while still meeting them at the right level of stakes.

## Why it's plausibly worth the read — and why fit matters more than usual here

Warikoo's strength in his other content is concreteness — numbered frameworks, specific scripts, an aversion to platitudes — and that translates reasonably well to a teenage audience that's often condescended to by either overly academic guidance counselors or overly saccharine self-help. He also has a large, demonstrated audience among young people in India specifically, which is some evidence the tone lands with the intended reader rather than just with the adults buying the book for them. The important caveat is fit: this is explicitly not his usual business/career/money content re-skinned — it's aimed at a materially younger audience with different problems (first relationships, parental conflict, exam pressure) than his other books address, so the value of reading it depends heavily on whether the reader (or the person it's being read for) is actually in that age band, rather than an adult expecting his usual startup-and-money material in a new cover.

## The honest heads-up

Books that promise to teach "what school doesn't" run a predictable risk: the material can default to generic confidence-and-money-basics advice that's true but not especially differentiated from other teen self-help, since concrete financial literacy content is genuinely hard to make specific without assuming a particular country's banking and job system, and "manage your emotions" advice compresses easily into platitudes at teen-book length. Warikoo's track record for directness is the main reason to expect this one to clear that bar better than average, but it's worth judging chapter by chapter whether the advice is a concrete script a teenager could actually use, or a restatement of "believe in yourself" with better production values.
