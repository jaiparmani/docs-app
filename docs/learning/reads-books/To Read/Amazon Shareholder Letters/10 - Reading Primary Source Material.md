# 10 — Reading Primary Source Material

<small>3 min read</small>

## Core idea
This collection is structurally different from most business books in one specific way worth naming directly: it isn't someone's reporting, biography, or synthesis of Amazon — it's a compilation of Bezos's own annual letters to shareholders, written contemporaneously as the company was actually operating, from 1997 through his final letters before stepping down as CEO. Every other theme in this set of notes — long-term thinking, customer obsession, Day 1, regret minimization, disagree and commit, two-way doors, two-pizza teams, the invention machine — comes directly from his own stated language in his own words, in the specific year he used it, rather than from an outside author's interpretation or reconstruction after the fact.

## Why it matters
That structural fact carries both the book's main value and its main limitation, and both follow directly from the same source. The value: you're reading the actual artifact institutional investors and employees read in real time, not a paraphrase — which means you can trace exactly when and how a given piece of language (like "Day 1" or "regret minimization") first appeared and how consistently it was carried forward across two decades, giving unmediated access to how Bezos actually reasoned about the company as events were unfolding rather than how a biographer later reconstructed that reasoning with the benefit of hindsight. The limitation: a shareholder letter is, by definition, written by a founder to an audience of investors he needs to keep supportive of decisions that often depress near-term earnings — it was never going to include independent reporting on what didn't work, interviews with employees who disagreed, or acknowledgment of controversies playing out at the time. The letters are also, unavoidably, repetitive in the way annual corporate letters tend to be, since much of their function is ritual reaffirmation of a stated philosophy rather than new argument each year.

## Example from the book
The clearest illustration of both sides at once is the 1997 letter's reprint tradition: appending the original letter to every subsequent year's letter for roughly two decades is genuinely remarkable as primary-source continuity — few founders leave a paper trail that lets you check, in their own words, whether they held to a standard they set decades earlier. But it's also exactly the kind of repetition that makes the collection read less like a book with a developing argument and more like the same thesis restated against a changing backdrop each year — valuable as a reference to dip into by theme, less suited to being read cover to cover for narrative momentum.

## Practical application
Read this collection the way the structure suggests rather than against it: treat it as a reference document to consult by theme or by year rather than a narrative to read start to finish, and pair it with an independently reported account — The Everything Store covers many of the same years from a journalist's rather than a founder's vantage point — to get the perspective the letters structurally can't provide on their own.

## Something to sit with
> [!question]- A question to think about
> Knowing that every theme in this collection comes from a document written to persuade shareholders as much as to inform them, which of these ideas would you want to see corroborated by an independent, outside account before fully trusting it?
