# Thinking Strategically
*Avinash Dixit and Barry Nalebuff*

Broken into 12 concepts, one per chunk, each a 3-5 minute read. Each chunk covers the core idea, why it matters, a specific example from the book, a practical way to apply it, and a question to sit with. Read them in order — later chunks (bargaining, auctions) lean on the reasoning tools built in the earlier ones (backward induction, credibility).

## Concepts

1. [Games Are Everywhere](01%20-%20Games%20Are%20Everywhere.md) — what actually makes a situation strategic rather than a plain decision
2. [Look Forward, Reason Backward](02%20-%20Look%20Forward%2C%20Reason%20Backward.md) — solving sequential games by working from the last move to the first
3. [Credible Commitments](03%20-%20Credible%20Commitments.md) — why burning your own boats can be a rational move
4. [Making Threats and Promises Credible](04%20-%20Making%20Threats%20and%20Promises%20Credible.md) — a threat only works if the other side believes you'd actually carry it out
5. [The Prisoners Dilemma](05%20-%20The%20Prisoners%20Dilemma.md) — how individually rational choices produce a jointly bad outcome
6. [Repeated Games and Tit-for-Tat](06%20-%20Repeated%20Games%20and%20Tit-for-Tat.md) — why cooperation can emerge from pure self-interest, given repetition
7. [Mixed Strategies](07%20-%20Mixed%20Strategies.md) — why deliberate unpredictability is sometimes the rational plan
8. [Bargaining and Your Outside Option](08%20-%20Bargaining%20and%20Your%20Outside%20Option.md) — BATNA-style leverage, decades before the term existed
9. [Auctions and the Winners Curse](09%20-%20Auctions%20and%20the%20Winners%20Curse.md) — why winning a bid can itself be bad news
10. [Voting Paradoxes and Arrows Theorem](10%20-%20Voting%20Paradoxes%20and%20Arrows%20Theorem.md) — why no voting system can be perfectly fair
11. [Coordination Games](11%20-%20Coordination%20Games.md) — why some equilibria are self-fulfilling, from bank runs to keyboard standards
12. [A Denser Kind of Read](12%20-%20A%20Denser%20Kind%20of%20Read.md) — closing note on what this book trades for its rigor
