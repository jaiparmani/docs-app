# 08 — The Hustle

<small>2 min read</small>

## Core idea
As a teenager, Noah built an informal business burning and selling pirated CDs, eventually expanding into DJing and running sound at parties with a crew of friends. It started small and practical — he had access to a computer and a CD burner when few people around him did — and grew into a genuine income stream, run with real business logic: sourcing music, managing turnaround time, pricing for his specific customer base, and reinvesting proceeds into better equipment. It's presented not as a cute anecdote but as Noah's actual introduction to commerce, negotiation, and reading a market.

## Why it matters
This thread matters because it shows Noah's resourcefulness operating on a completely different axis from the language-switching and social navigation described elsewhere in the book — here it's applied to material survival and self-generated opportunity in an environment where formal paths to income were scarce or inaccessible. It also complicates any narrative that frames apartheid-era and post-apartheid townships purely as sites of deprivation; the informal economy Noah describes was resourceful, entrepreneurial, and in his account, a genuine source of pride and competence-building, not just a workaround born of desperation.

## Example from the book
Noah describes the specific mechanics of the CD business — the technical setup, the need to move product quickly before demand for a given hit shifted, and the negotiations involved in getting himself and his equipment into parties as a paid DJ rather than just a fan. He treats the hustle with real narrative energy and specificity: this isn't a vague gesture at "he was resourceful as a kid," it's an account of an actual functioning operation, complete with the kinds of judgment calls — who to trust, when to expand, when to cut losses — that any small business owner would recognize.

## Practical application
As a way of thinking about resourcefulness, the chapter suggests treating whatever asymmetric access you happen to have — a skill, a piece of equipment, a piece of information others in your environment lack — as a starting point for building something, rather than waiting for a more legible or conventional opportunity to present itself. Noah didn't wait for a formal job market that had little use for a mixed-race teenager without connections; he started from what he already had access to and built outward from there, learning the underlying business skills — pricing, cash flow, reputation — along the way rather than in a classroom.

## Something to sit with
> [!question]- A question to think about
> What's an asymmetric piece of access you already have — a skill, a tool, a piece of local knowledge — that you've been treating as incidental rather than as a starting point for something?
