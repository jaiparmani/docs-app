# 04 — Three Churches Every Sunday

<small>2 min read</small>

## Core idea
Patricia's Christianity is not a background detail in the book — it's one of the central organizing forces of Noah's childhood. He describes a Sunday routine that involved attending not one but three different church services in a row: a mixed-race church, a white church, and a Black church, each with its own style of worship, its own congregation, and its own social codes. It wasn't casual devotion; it was a full-day commitment she imposed without much room for negotiation, treated as non-negotiable as school itself.

## Why it matters
The three-church Sunday is one of the clearest windows the book offers into how deliberately Patricia raised her son. She wasn't simply devout — she was using faith and its institutions as a tool to expose Trevor to as many different worlds as she could fit into a single day, and to make sure he never became fluent in only one register of South African life. Given everything else in the book about how apartheid tried to keep worlds separate, this reads as a quiet, persistent act of defiance: a mother using her own institution-hopping to make sure her son's world stayed wider than the one the state had designed for people like him.

## Example from the book
Noah recounts the physical and emotional exhaustion of the ritual — three services in different neighborhoods, different musical traditions, different lengths, different expectations for how a child should behave — undertaken as a matter of routine, not occasion. He describes it with the mix of exasperation and affection typical of the book's voice toward his mother throughout: it was genuinely a lot to ask of a child, and also, in hindsight, clearly part of a larger design he only came to appreciate later, once he understood how unusual it was for a Black South African family to move that fluidly across those particular congregations.

## Practical application
As a way of thinking about raising or shaping a person's worldview, the three-church Sunday suggests that exposure itself — repeated, structured, even uncomfortable exposure to different rooms full of different people — does more to widen someone's sense of what's normal than any amount of explanation could. Patricia didn't sit Trevor down and explain pluralism to him; she put him in three different rooms every week until moving between them was just how Sundays worked. The application isn't literally about church — it's about treating exposure to different worlds as a deliberate, repeatable practice rather than something that happens to a person by accident.

## Something to sit with
> [!question]- A question to think about
> What's a "third church" you could add to your own routine — a room full of people unlike your usual rooms, visited regularly enough that it stops feeling like an event and starts feeling normal?
