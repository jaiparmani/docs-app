# 09 — Temptation Bundling and Structuring Your Environment

<small>3 min read</small>

## Core idea
Rather than treating self-control as a matter of resisting temptation through effort in the moment, you can restructure the situation so the good choice requires no resistance at all. Temptation bundling is one specific version of this: pairing an activity you should do but don't particularly want to with an activity you want to do but feel you shouldn't spend as much time on, so the two are only available together — the indulgent activity becomes the reward that's contingent on doing the effortful one. More broadly, this is one instance of a wider principle: motivation problems are often solvable by redesigning the environment or the choice architecture around a behavior, rather than by trying to out-willpower it.

## Why it matters
Willpower-based self-control has a well-documented cost: resisting temptation in the moment, over and over, is effortful and unreliable, and it fails more often than any of us would like to admit, especially when we're tired, stressed, or depleted from resisting something else earlier in the day. Structural fixes don't have this failure mode in the same way, because they don't ask you to resist anything in the moment — the tempting and the effortful activity are simply fused, or the environment is arranged so the desired behavior is the path of least resistance and the undesired one requires active extra effort to reach.

## Example from the book
The clearest evidence for temptation bundling comes from a field experiment by Katy Milkman and colleagues at a university gym: researchers gave one group of participants iPods loaded with captivating audiobooks — the kind of page-turner fiction people struggle to put down — but restricted access to those audiobooks to gym workout sessions only. A comparison group had unrestricted access to the same audiobooks anywhere. The gym-only group showed a meaningfully higher increase in voluntary gym attendance than the group that could listen to the same books at home, on their commute, or anywhere else. Nothing about the workout itself changed; what changed was that the enjoyable activity had been made conditional on the effortful one, so choosing to skip the gym now also meant giving up the audiobook, and choosing to work out delivered its own immediate, unrelated pleasure alongside whatever fitness benefit would arrive later.

## Practical application
Identify an indulgence you currently enjoy freely, with no conditions attached, and see whether it can be restricted to pair with a should-do activity instead — a favorite podcast or show only during a workout, a favorite coffee shop treat only while doing a task you've been avoiding, a call with a friend you enjoy only while doing household chores you don't. Separately, look for structural, no-willpower-required fixes to your environment: removing a tempting app from your phone's home screen, laying out running clothes the night before, or keeping unhealthy snacks physically out of the house rather than relying on declining them anew each time you see them.
