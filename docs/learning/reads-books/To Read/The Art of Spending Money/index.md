# The Art of Spending Money
*Morgan Housel*

Housel's 2025 book turns his behavioral-finance lens away from earning and investing — the subject of *The Psychology of Money* — and points it at the side of the ledger that gets the least attention relative to how often it's actually exercised: what to do with money once it's yours. This is a newer, less widely documented title than his earlier books, so these notes stay more thematic and general than a well-worn classic would allow — a known and expected limit given how recently it published, not a gap in the reading.

## Concepts

1. [Spending Is a Skill of Its Own](01%20-%20Spending%20Is%20a%20Skill%20of%20Its%20Own.md) — why spending well is a separate competency from earning or investing well
2. [The Third Piece of a Loose Trilogy](02%20-%20The%20Third%20Piece%20of%20a%20Loose%20Trilogy.md) — how this book completes the arc from *The Psychology of Money* and *Same as Ever*
3. [Most Purchases Are Signals in Disguise](03%20-%20Most%20Purchases%20Are%20Signals%20in%20Disguise.md) — why "practical" purchases are often status purchases wearing a disguise
4. [The Treadmill That Resets Your Baseline](04%20-%20The%20Treadmill%20That%20Resets%20Your%20Baseline.md) — why upgrades deliver a spike, not a lasting lift
5. [Enough Is a Moving Target](05%20-%20Enough%20Is%20a%20Moving%20Target.md) — why "enough" has to be decided, not discovered
6. [Spending as a Statement of Values](06%20-%20Spending%20as%20a%20Statement%20of%20Values.md) — why there's no universal optimal, only your optimal
7. [Buying Back Your Time and Attention](07%20-%20Buying%20Back%20Your%20Time%20and%20Attention.md) — the one category of spending that seems to keep paying off
8. [The Real Skill Is Knowing Yourself](08%20-%20The%20Real%20Skill%20Is%20Knowing%20Yourself.md) — closing synthesis: why no formula can replace self-knowledge
