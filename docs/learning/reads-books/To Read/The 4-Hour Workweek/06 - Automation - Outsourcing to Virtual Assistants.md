# 06 — Automation: Outsourcing to Virtual Assistants

<small>2 min read</small>

## Core idea
Once the unnecessary work has been eliminated, Ferriss's automation step is largely about removing yourself as the bottleneck for what remains — both in business operations and in personal life — by handing tasks to remote virtual assistants rather than doing them personally or hiring in-house staff. The pitch is not limited to business tasks: he extends outsourcing to personal errands and administrative overhead on the logic that any recurring task that doesn't require your specific judgment is a candidate for delegation, regardless of whether it's labeled "work."

## Why it matters
Most people mentally sort tasks into "things only I can do" and "things I do anyway," without examining whether the second category is accurate. Ferriss's argument is that a large share of daily tasks — scheduling, research, routine correspondence, even things like gift shopping or bill paying — don't actually require the specific person doing them, they've just always been done by that person. Outsourcing these, even at modest cost, buys back hours that can be redirected toward the small number of tasks that do require your particular skill or judgment, which is a very different trade than it appears to be if you only account for the dollar cost and not the freed time.

## Example from the book
Ferriss describes hiring overseas virtual assistants — sourcing them through outsourcing firms based in India such as those he references by name (AY Outsourcing and Brickwork among them) — to handle tasks ranging from email triage and research to distinctly personal errands like arranging a gift or handling a customer service call. He frames this partly as a business efficiency and partly as a test of a broader premise: that once a task is clearly specified, the person completing it doesn't need to be you, or even in the same country as you.

## Practical application
List three recurring tasks you currently do yourself that don't require your unique judgment — routine scheduling, research, form-filling, correspondence. For one of them, write a clear enough specification that someone else could complete it without you present, and test delegating it, even informally, before assuming it has to stay on your plate.

## Something to sit with
> [!question]- A question to think about
> Which task on your plate right now do you keep doing yourself less because it requires you specifically, and more because delegating it would require writing down exactly how it's done?
